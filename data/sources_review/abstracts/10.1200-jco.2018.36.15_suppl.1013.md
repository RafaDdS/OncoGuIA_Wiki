# Final overall survival (OS) analysis of PHEREXA: A randomized phase III trial of trastuzumab (H) + capecitabine (X) ± pertuzumab (P) in patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer (MBC) who experienced disease progression during or after H-based therapy.

**Autores:** Urruticoechea Ander; Rizwanullah Mohammed; Im Seock-Ah; S&#225;nchez Ruiz Antonio Carlos; Lang Istvan; Tomasello Gianluca; Douthwaite Hannah; Badovinac Crnjevic Tanja; Heeson Sarah; Eng-Wong Jennifer; Munoz Montserrat
**Fonte:** Journal of Clinical Oncology; 2018;36(15_suppl):1013 (Abstract LBA/1013, ASCO 2018)
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1200/jco.2018.36.15_suppl.1013
**NCT:** NCT01026142

## Resumo (Abstract) — extraído do poster do ASCO 2018 (publica&#231;&#227;o aberta)

### Background
- H + chemotherapy + P is highly effective for the first-line treatment of HER2-positive MBC and for high-risk HER2-positive early BC.
- The PHEREXA study (NCT01026142) evaluated H + X &#177; P in patients with HER2-positive MBC who received a prior taxane and progressed during or after H-based therapy.
- The study rationale was based on the German Breast Group 26 trial, which showed that continuing H with X after progressing on an H-containing regimen increased response and time to progression compared with X alone, without an increase in toxicity.
- In addition, comprehensive HER2 signaling blockade with H + P has been shown to be active in patients who received prior H therapy.
- In the primary analysis of PHEREXA (clinical cutoff: May 29, 2015), adding P to H + X did not significantly improve independent review facility-assessed progression-free survival (IRF-PFS; the primary endpoint): IRF-PFS HR 0.82; 95% CI 0.65&#8211;1.02; p = .0731.
- An 8-month increase in median OS (secondary endpoint) from 28.1 to 36.1 months was observed with P at an interim OS analysis at the time of the primary IRF-PFS analysis, but due to hierarchical testing of IRF-PFS, and subsequently of OS (to control type I error), statistical significance could not be claimed.
- No new safety signals were identified. We now report the final prespecified analysis of the secondary endpoints of OS, investigator-assessed PFS (INV-PFS), and safety.

### Methods
- **Study design:** PHEREXA is a multicenter, open-label, randomized phase III study.
- H and P were given intravenously every 3 weeks. X was given orally twice daily (2 weeks on, 1 week off, every 3 weeks).
- Arms (centrally confirmed HER2-positive MBC, prior taxane and H, progression during or after H-based therapy for MBC): H IV (8 mg/kg&#8594;6 mg/kg) + X PO (1,250 mg/m&#178;) (n = 224) vs H IV (8 mg/kg&#8594;6 mg/kg) + X PO (1,000 mg/m&#178;) + P IV (840 mg&#8594;420 mg) (n = 228). N = 452.
- First patient included Jan 26, 2010; last patient included Aug 12, 2013. Treatment was given until disease progression, unmanageable toxicity, or patient request for discontinuation.
- Stratification was by prior central nervous system disease, measurable vs nonmeasurable disease, and response to first-line H.
- OS and INV-PFS were assessed in the intention-to-treat population (all randomly assigned patients). Median INV-PFS estimated using the Kaplan-Meier approach; hazard ratios between arms and 95% CI using the stratified Cox proportional hazards regression model (stratified by random assignment stratification factors).
- Adverse events (AEs) were assessed in the safety population (patients who received &#8805;1 dose of study drug).

### Results
- Clinical cutoff was Sep 20, 2017. Median time on study, including post-treatment follow-up, was 23.2 months in the H + X arm and 33.0 months in the H + X + P arm.
- Demographics and baseline characteristics were generally similar between arms. Slightly over half of patients (55%) had hormone receptor-positive disease; 65% had visceral disease; prior H exposure was generally comparable between arms. All patients received first-line H for MBC; 25% also received H in the early setting. Approximately 40% of patients had &gt;12 months' response duration in the first line (good responders to H).
- **Final OS:** HR 0.76 (95% CI 0.60&#8211;0.98); median OS 28.1 months (H + X) vs 37.2 months (H + X + P) (&#916; = 9.1 months). Although the increase in median OS was clinically meaningful, this was **not** statistically significant due to hierarchical testing.
- **INV-PFS:** HR 0.83 (95% CI 0.68&#8211;1.02); median PFS 9.0 months vs 11.8 months (&#916; = 2.8 months); events 182/224 (81.3%) vs 201/228 (88.2%).
- Total deaths: 129 (59.2%) vs 127 (55.7%).
- **Safety:** Any AE 98.2% vs 97.4%; NCI-CTCAE grade &#8805;3 60.1% vs 53.5%; serious AE 24.3% vs 25.4%; AE leading to treatment discontinuation 19.3% vs 21.9%; AE resulting in death 2 (0.9%) vs 1 (0.4%). Common AEs: diarrhea 59.6% vs 69.7%; hand&#8211;foot syndrome 73.4% vs 56.6%; nausea 45.0% vs 38.6%; rash 5.0% vs 15.8%; neutropenia 17.9% vs 13.2%.
- **Cardiac safety:** Symptomatic LVSD 0 vs 5 (2.2%); NYHA Class II 0 vs 3 (1.3), Class III 0 vs 1, Class IV 0 vs 1; asymptomatic LVEF drops &#8805;10 points below baseline and value &lt;50%: 6 (2.8%) vs 13 (5.7%); LVEF drop requiring treatment or leading to discontinuation: 1 (0.5%) vs 1 (0.4%).

### Conclusions
- Although the primary endpoint was not met, with a median OS of 37.2 months in the H + X + P arm vs 28.1 months in the H + X arm (a 9.1-month increase), the final analysis of OS in PHEREXA was consistent with the interim OS analysis and supports clinical activity of H + P being maintained with longer follow-up.
- INV-PFS was consistent with the primary analysis, with median of 9.0 months in the H + X arm and 11.8 months in the H + X + P arm.
- Overall, the safety profile of the H + X + P regimen was consistent with previous studies of P. No new safety signals were observed.
- The incidence of symptomatic LVSD did not change from the primary analysis, which confirms the existing cardiac safety profile of H + P. There was no evidence of late cardiac toxicity.
